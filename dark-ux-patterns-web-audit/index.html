Abuse of Platform Power & Algorithmic Asymmetry

Draft · Karina Lujan · Garland, TX · October 2026

01 The argument

Centralized social platforms are built to favor ad revenue and engagement over user safety, accessibility, and the reach of creators’ work.

This paper lays out that argument and the audit we use to test it. It is a draft: the claims below are hypotheses, and findings will be added as the audits are run.

02 Architectural asymmetry

Every platform has two kinds of paths. Revenue paths earn it money: buying an ad, boosting a post, upgrading an account. Protection paths keep people safe and included: reporting a post, blocking an account, turning on captions or reduced motion.

Our hypothesis is that revenue paths get more investment than protection paths: fewer steps, faster responses, better keyboard and screen reader support, and a more visible place in the interface. If that is true, the gap should be measurable from the outside, by anyone, without access to a platform’s servers.

03 How we test it

Each measure pairs a revenue path with a protection path on the same platform, tested on the same day and device.
